12 Websites with Confusing Design

A list of twelve sites that make reading hard.

Morgan Reyes · March 4, 2025

Want to see what makes a website hard to read? This list walks through twelve common mistakes, with what each one costs a reader and how a designer could fix it.

1. Flashing banners

Moving and blinking elements pull the eye away from the text. Readers with attention difficulties lose their place every few seconds, and many readers simply leave the page within the first minute.

The fix is simple: keep motion for feedback, never for decoration, and respect the reduced-motion setting that the operating system already offers to every visitor.

2. Tiny grey text

Light grey text at twelve pixels on a white background fails contrast guidelines and tires the eyes. Body text should be at least sixteen pixels, and the contrast ratio should be at least 4.5 to 1.

Designers often pick grey because it looks calm in a mock-up. On a real screen, in daylight, on a cheap laptop, the same grey turns into a blur that nobody wants to read for long.

3. Endless pop-ups

A newsletter pop-up, then a cookie banner, then a chat bubble: three interruptions before the first sentence. Each one hides the content the reader came for and asks for a decision they did not plan to make.

Ask for one thing at a time, after the reader has seen some of the page, and make the close button large and easy to find with a keyboard.

4. Walls of text

Paragraphs that run for twenty lines without a break are hard to follow. Short paragraphs, clear headings and lists help readers scan, and help readers with dyslexia keep their place on the line.

5. Lines that never end

On a wide monitor, a line of text can stretch to two hundred characters. The eye has to travel too far to find the start of the next line. Between fifty and ninety characters per line is comfortable for most readers.
